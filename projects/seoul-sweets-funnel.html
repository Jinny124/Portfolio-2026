Seoul Sweets Funnel Analysis

A customer-journey funnel analysis for an online store's first day of delivery service — mapping where 10 users dropped off between viewing a product and completing payment, and what to fix first.

Overview

What we had to work with

Seoul Sweets launched its online store and delivery service on 1 August 2024. This analysis covers the activity log from that first day — 10 users, 29 activity rows, tracked across 4 funnel stages, built into a pivot table and funnel chart in Google Sheets.

Problem statement

Only 30% of users complete payment

Of 10 users who viewed a product, only 3 reached payment — a 30% overall conversion rate. The biggest leak sits at the very last step: of 7 users who filled in delivery info, 4 never finished paying. Seoul Sweets didn't yet know where users stopped or why, so it couldn't prioritize fixes on its new store.

Why it matters
  • The store just launched — day-one drop-off becomes the baseline for every fix after this.
  • Acquisition cost is already spent, but 70% of users produce no revenue.
  • Users who reach Delivery Info have the highest intent to buy — the most expensive kind to lose.
Funnel chart

Where users drop off

Built in Google Sheets: a pivot table flags which stage each user reached, turned into this funnel. The steepest drop is the last step — checkout to payment.

Actionable vs. vanity

Which metric should drive the fix?

An actionable metric answers “what's the next move?” — a vanity metric only answers “how busy are we?”

Actionable metric

Delivery Info → Confirm Payment = 3/7 = 42.9%

  • Points to one clear stage — the team knows exactly what to fix.
  • Moves directly into action: add payment methods, show shipping cost earlier, fix payment errors.
  • Can be a measurable target — e.g. 42.9% → 70% within a month.
Vanity metric

Total Product View

10 users viewed a product = 100% of traffic

  • Looks good and keeps climbing with traffic, but says nothing about what to fix.
  • Disconnected from revenue: 10 views still produced only 3 orders.
  • Only useful as the funnel's denominator, not as a success indicator.
